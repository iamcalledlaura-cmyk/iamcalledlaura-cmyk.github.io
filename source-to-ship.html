Source to ship: converting live training with an AI-assisted pipeline

A new learning platform opened the door to growth that live courses couldn’t reach. I’m leading the conversion of six multi-day instructor-led courses into fully self-paced eLearning, using a pipeline where AI drafts and a human gate decides what ships at every stage.

Why now

Live training had a ceiling

Every course needed a facilitator, a calendar slot, and enough learners in the same time zone to fill a room, so growth meant adding facilitators. Moving to a new learning platform changed that. Self-paced courses reach learners anywhere, at any time, without adding delivery cost for each new cohort.

That made conversion a business priority: more learners reached, faster enrollment, and new revenue from courses that delivery capacity used to cap. The challenge was speed. Converting six week-long courses by hand would take months, and straight conversions tend to become narrated slide decks that lose everything that made the live sessions work.

Seven stages, five ADDIE phases, a human gate at each one

Where AI helps most

AI does the heaviest lifting in Analyze and Develop: audits, content maps, scripts, and translations. Human judgment carries more of the weight in Design and Evaluate, where the decisions are about what learners need and what the data actually means.

Tools: Claude for analysis and drafting, Articulate Rise for course builds, ElevenLabs for voiceover, vibe-coded simulations embedded in Rise, xAPI for learning data, Zapier for workflow automation, and dashboards built with Claude Code.

Where the time savings come from

  • No logistics time: introductions, breaks, waiting for the group, and technical setup
  • Learners move at their own pace instead of the group’s
  • A pre-check lets experienced learners skip what they already know
  • Common questions are answered once, inside the content, instead of in every session
  • Practice time goes to the skills that need it most
Replacing the role-play

The hardest thing to convert

In a live escalation course, role-play is where people build the skill. A facilitator plays the angry customer, watches the response, and coaches in the moment. Without a facilitator, two kinds of self-paced practice take its place.

Branching scenario

  • Choosing the right move as a customer’s frustration builds over several turns
  • Each choice changes how the customer reacts
  • Feedback explains why each path worked or failed

AI customer simulation

  • Learners type their own replies to an AI-played customer
  • A strong reply calms the customer; a scripted or defensive one makes things worse
  • Practice in the learner’s own words, which multiple choice can’t give

Where I steered

The small calls that shape what learners get

Because every stage is gated, AI drafts rarely ship with outright errors. The real work is noticing when a draft is technically fine but wrong for the learner, and steering it somewhere better. Four examples from the Day 2 build:

The objective that only asked learners to remember

“Learners will be able to list the four stages of the escalation cycle.”
Knowing the stages is useless if you can’t spot them on a live call. Rewrite at the apply level.
“Given a customer conversation, identify which stage the customer is in and choose a response that fits it.”

The scenario that could happen anywhere

“A customer is upset about a problem with their order and wants it fixed.”
Too generic to feel real. Give the customer a specific situation, a history, and a reason to be angry.
“This is Dana’s third contact about a refund promised ten days ago. The last agent said it was processed. It wasn’t.”

The voiceover script that ran long

A 260-word narration covering every point from the facilitator guide.
Cut to what the learner needs to act on, move detail to the job aid, and let the interaction carry the teaching.
A 110-word script, with the remaining detail in a downloadable job aid.

The distractors nobody would pick

Wrong answers that were obviously rude, like “Tell the customer to calm down.”
Real mistakes sound reasonable. Make the wrong answers what well-meaning agents actually say.
“I completely understand your frustration” (scripted) and “Let me transfer you” (passing ownership).

Build QA (Stage 4)

  • Content matches the approved storyboard and source material
  • Captions and transcripts for all audio and video
  • Color contrast, keyboard navigation, and alt text meet WCAG guidelines
  • Every branch of each scenario tested end to end
  • Job aids match the final course content
Pilot and launch

Every promise traces back to the design

I advise marketing and sales on how to position each course, and review launch materials for accuracy. AI-written marketing copy drifts toward hype, so the gate is simple: every claim must trace back to a learning objective or a design decision. Each course then goes to a pilot group first, and their feedback shapes adjustments before full rollout.

Measure and iterate

AI finds the patterns. I decide what they mean.
