chapter 3

3 frameworks every draft has to meet

AI drafts default to “explain, then quiz.” These 3 push them toward practice. I build them into the prompts and the review rubric.

Merrill's First Principles

Start with a real problem

Learning sticks when it starts with a real task, shows how, then lets people try it.

In this courseEvery module opens on Sam's score drop, shows a model conversation, then hands you the controls.

Gagné's 9 Events

Teach in the right order

9 steps for any lesson: hook them, set the goal, teach, guide, practice, give feedback, check and help it transfer.

In this courseEach 25-minute module climbs the same 9 steps, ending with a coaching plan for next week.

Mayer's multimedia principles

Cut what doesn't help

People learn more from lean screens: short chunks, plain words and narration that sounds human.

In this courseScripts dropped from 260 to 110 words. The extra detail moved to a job aid.

    chapter 7

    4 fixes with a red pen

    The 4 rubric fails from chapter 4, and what I changed.

    The objective

    Merrill · Apply

    “Learners will be able to list the 4 steps of the coaching model.”

    They need to run the conversation, with real data in hand.

    “Given an agent's QA results, plan and open a coaching conversation that targets the biggest gap.”

    The scenario

    Merrill · Problem

    “An agent's quality score is low.”

    Give him a name, a trend and a pattern to find.

    “Sam dropped from 88 to 72 in a month. 3 of his last 5 calls missed the same step.”

    The voiceover

    Mayer · Coherence

    260 words covering the whole facilitator guide.

    Keep what they'll act on. Move the rest.

    110 words, plus a job aid.

    The wrong answers

    Real practice

    Options so harsh anyone would spot them.

    Real mistakes sound reasonable.

    “Your score is 72, the target is 85” (data, no direction) and “Everyone has a bad month” (avoiding it).

    Expectation

    Press a button, get a finished course.

    Reality

    AI drafts fast. I edit until it fits the learner.

    chapter 8

    Climbing the evidence ladder

    A completion rate only proves people showed up. I plan to measure higher up the ladder, all the way to “their team's scores went up.”

    Showed upCompletions
    Clicked throughTime, retries, drop-off
    Liked itPilot survey
    Knows itKnowledge checks
    Makes good callsScenario choices
    Can do itAI coaching sim, scored
    Does it on the jobCoaching quality on the floor
    It made a differenceTeam QA scores
    TrackedMeasured in the pilotAfter launch

    Built on Will Thalheimer's LTEM model. It maps to Kirkpatrick too: “Liked it” is level 1, the middle 3 steps are level 2, “Does it on the job” is level 3 and “It made a difference” is level 4.

    Draft finds patterns. I decide what they mean.

    Most opened with the score at 1 pointI added a model conversation right before it.
    Drop-off spiked mid-moduleI split it in 2.
    Everyone missed 1 questionI rewrote the question and left the content alone.
