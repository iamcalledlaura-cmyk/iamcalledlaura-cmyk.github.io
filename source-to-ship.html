Why now

Live training had a ceiling

Every live course needed a facilitator, a time slot, and a full room. A new learning platform meant courses could reach anyone, anytime, and bring in revenue that live delivery capped.

The catch: converting six courses by hand would take months, and quick conversions usually end up as narrated slide decks.

The pipeline

Seven stages, five ADDIE phases, a human gate at each one

Replacing the role-play

The hardest thing to convert

Role-play is where people actually learn to calm someone down. With no facilitator, two kinds of practice take its place.

Recorded role-play video

Learners watch an expert agent handle an escalation, then answer a short quiz.

Q3. What should the agent do first?a) Apologizeb) Transfer the callc) Escalate to a supervisord) All of the above

Branching scenario

  • Pick a reply as the customer gets angrier
  • Each choice changes how they react

AI customer simulation

  • Type your own reply to an AI customer
  • Good replies calm them down; scripted ones don’t

Where I steered

The small calls that shape what learners get

AI drafts are rarely wrong. They’re often just not quite right for the learner. Four small calls from the Day 2 build:

The objective that only asked learners to remember

“Learners will be able to list the four stages of the escalation cycle.”
Knowing the stages doesn’t help on a live call. Make it about spotting them.
“Given a customer conversation, identify which stage the customer is in and choose a response that fits it.”

The scenario that could happen anywhere

“A customer is upset about a problem with their order and wants it fixed.”
Too generic. Give her a name, a history, and a reason to be angry.
“This is Dana’s third contact about a refund promised ten days ago. The last agent said it was processed. It wasn’t.”

The voiceover script that ran long

260 words covering everything in the facilitator guide.
Keep only what they need to act on. Move the rest to the job aid.
110 words, plus a downloadable job aid.

The distractors nobody would pick

Wrong answers so rude nobody would pick them.
Real mistakes sound polite. Use what agents actually say.
“I completely understand your frustration” (scripted) and “Let me transfer you” (passing ownership).
Quality gates

What every draft has to pass

All content undergoes robust quality assurance processes to ensure accuracy, engagement and alignment with instructional design best practices.

Design review (Stage 3)What I check
ObjectivesAbout doing, and each one gets practiced
FacilitationEvery live moment has a replacement, or a reason it was cut
RelevanceScenarios feel like the real job
LengthModules under 30 minutes
AccuracyEvery claim traces to a source or an SME
InclusionWorks across regions and languages

Before launch, every build also gets an accessibility check (WCAG) and every scenario branch is tested end to end.

Pilot and launch

Every promise traces back to the design

I advise marketing and sales on positioning and check their launch copy. My one rule: every claim has to match something the course actually does. Then a pilot group goes first, and I adjust before full rollout.
