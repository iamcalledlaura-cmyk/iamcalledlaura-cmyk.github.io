Turning complex regulation into human-centered learning at scale, so support advisors could act on privacy rights in real customer conversations.

The problem

Advisors were applying the law in real time, from legal language

Support advisors had to carry out GDPR and CCPA requirements live, across millions of global interactions. But privacy guidance lived in legal language and fragmented documentation.

  • Low confidence deciding when to self-serve and when to escalate
  • Inconsistent handling across regions
  • Higher compliance risk
  • Friction in conversations with users
Process

Define the behavior first, then the content

I used a human-centered, design-thinking approach to translate business objectives into learner outcomes, partnering with Legal, Operations, and Support leaders.

Define desired behaviors

What an advisor must do in the conversation, not just what they must know.

Clarify legal workflows with SMEs

Interviews with Legal and Privacy to turn requirements into decision points.

Review real tickets

Find exactly where handling broke down in live customer interactions.

Listen to advisors

Gather feedback on friction and confidence gaps, then write problem statements and experience principles to guide the build.

The solution

The Privacy League: practice on the requests advisors actually get

I designed a month-long Privacy Month experience built around the Privacy League, a three-mission series that casts every advisor as a guardian of user trust. Each mission blends a short animated briefing with hands-on practice. Rather than presenting policy, every module focuses on doing the work.

Foundations Briefing

Why privacy matters, the GDPR and CCPA rights behind every case, and the advisor's role on the frontline of trust. About 10 minutes.

Privacy in Practice

Applying the rights to the real requests advisors handle every day.

Mission Confidential

Real-world judgment: spotting personal data, redacting risky messages, and catching slip-ups before they happen. About 15 minutes.

Key elements

  • Modular learning journeys in Rise and Skilljar
  • Scenario simulations modeled on real tickets
  • Embedded workflows and escalation paths
  • Badges, leaderboards, and recognition
  • Advisor-facing rollout communications

Requests learners practiced

  • “Delete all my Spotify data”
  • “Stop using my data for ads”
  • “My birthdate is wrong”
  • “Can I download my listening history?”

Each scenario reinforced both the correct action and empathetic communication.

Impact

Fewer errors, and a framework that got reused

  • 100% participation across Customer and Creator Support, 2,000+ learners
  • Clearer judgment on when to self-serve and when to escalate
  • Critical privacy errors fell 25% in the first month, and the improvement held over the following year
  • Built in-house, avoiding $125K+ in external development costs
  • A scalable framework later reused for other compliance initiatives

More importantly, advisors began treating privacy as part of delivering a great user experience, not just legal compliance.

How the exposure estimate was modeled

The $185K+ figure is an estimate of reduced regulatory exposure, not realized savings. It assumes one million monthly contacts, a representative 5% QA sample, a 5% baseline failure rate, the measured 25% relative reduction, a 0.05% escalation rate, and a $2,500 per-violation benchmark.
