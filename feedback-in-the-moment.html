Feedback in the Moment

A scenario-based learning experience that helps Netflix employees build everyday feedback habits, one moment at a time.

Learning experience designer
Design exercise
1 week
Docebo LMS
React, Claude
The challenge

Feedback was only happening during formal reviews

Netflix's Learning and People Development team had a problem: employees were only engaging with feedback during formal 360 review cycles. Outside those windows, everyday feedback, the kind that actually shifts behavior, wasn't happening.

Their existing resources weren't solving it. The Feedback Workshop was a one-size-fits-all session. The Feedback Toolkit was comprehensive but overwhelming. Neither met people in the flow of their actual work.

The brief

Design a single digital learning experience, 5 to 7 minutes, mobile-responsive, hosted in Docebo, that helps employees build real feedback habits across four global regions with meaningfully different cultural norms.

Research and insight

The gap isn't knowledge. It's action.

Netflix provided sentiment data from employees across all four regions. A few patterns stood out: engagement with feedback tools dropped sharply outside 360 cycles, employees reported a gap between wanting to give feedback and actually doing it, and the reasons varied by person, not just by region. A new hire in London and a director in São Paulo might avoid feedback for completely different reasons, or for the same one.

Before designing anything, I mapped the honest scope of what a learning experience could move, and what it couldn't.

What learning can change

  • A simple, repeatable approach for in-the-moment feedback
  • Practice reps in realistic, culturally nuanced contexts
  • Adapting style across relationships, power dynamics, and cultural norms

What needs the wider business

  • Feedback infrastructure that lasts beyond 360 cycles
  • Managers modeling the behavior by asking for feedback, not just giving it
  • Feedback woven into rituals like 1:1s, retros, and milestones

This distinction matters. A five-minute digital experience won't single-handedly shift a culture. But it can be the strongest lever L&D owns, if we're honest about the dependencies.

Design approach

A four-week system, not a single course

Instead of another workshop or toolkit, I designed a four-week spaced learning system delivered through Slack into Docebo. Each week, a notification drops the learner into one 5 to 7 minute scenario-based module.

Week 1 · prototyped

Give it

Initiating feedback in a real moment, even when it feels uncomfortable.

Week 2

Receive it

Hearing hard feedback without shutting down or getting defensive.

Week 3

Seek it

Actively asking for the feedback you're not getting.

Week 4

Do it

Self-directed practice: a real conversation, supported but not scaffolded.

Spaced, not all at onceA single session doesn't build a habit. Weekly pacing leaves time to practice in the real world between modules.
Scenario-based, not theoreticalEvery module anchors to a recognizable workplace moment. Learners make choices instead of reading about them.
Adaptive by barrier, not by regionA check-in asks learners to name their biggest barrier, then routes them to the matching scenario path.
Bridges to real actionEvery module ends with a concrete commitment. Learning is complete when they have the conversation.

Key design decisions

Four choices that shaped the experience

Netflix IP over generic scenarios

An emotional hook only Netflix has

Clips from Emily in Paris make it feel like a Netflix product, not a training module.

Slack over LMS notifications

Meet people where they work

Practice doesn't feel like a separate task, and Docebo's native Slack integration makes it feasible.

Weekly pacing over self-paced

Time to have the real conversation

One module a week leaves room to practice before the next skill drops.

Self-selection over assigned paths

Route by need, not demographics

Two very different employees who name the same barrier get the same path, because their need is the same.

What I'd do next

This prototype is module 1 of 4

Complete the system

Build Receive it, Seek it, and Do it to the same depth, each with its own scenarios and clips.

Partner for sustainment

After week 4, shift to micro-prompts in standups, 1:1s, and retros, in partnership with People Management and Operations.

Build the measurement layer

Track behavior, not completion: are feedback conversations happening more often, and are barrier patterns shifting? Pair Docebo xAPI data with sentiment data.

Pilot, then scale

Run one region first and use real learner data to tune scenarios, pacing, and routing before going global.
