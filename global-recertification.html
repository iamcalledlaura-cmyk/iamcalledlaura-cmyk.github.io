My role

One owner, from problem definition to the last retest

I owned this end to end: problem definition, learning design, content, exam design, platform build, the translation program, rollout guidelines, the results and analytics pipeline, and the iteration cycles after launch. I worked with the standards committee, regional leaders, regional training leads, and marketing, and stayed the operational owner through the full completion window.

The problem

A substantial revision, a certified population, a hard deadline

The new release was a significant rewrite of the standard. It introduced a new management framework, a new model for classifying who performs work, metrics chosen by intent rather than fixed lists, and a rewritten hierarchy of core terms.

Everyone certified on the previous release, internal consultants and a global client base, had to be current on the new one or lose accreditation. The only existing route was a multi-day class.

Risks

  • Accreditation lapsing across a global population at a fixed deadline
  • Changes scattered across the whole standard; a summary isn't the same as being able to apply it

 

  • Multiple languages, with very different regional training capacity
  • Credibility: an inconsistent or badly translated exam damages the standard itself
Process

A behavior problem, not a content problem

A certified leader doesn't need to recite the changes. They need to place a piece of work in the right phase of the new framework, classify a performer correctly, and choose metrics by intent.

Define the smallest set of new behaviors

With the standards committee and regional leaders: what must a previously certified leader do differently now?

Design for the delta only

Assume fluency in the previous release and never re-teach the foundation. That kept it under two hours.

Pilot internally first

Consultants across regions ran the full training and exam over two weeks before any client saw it.

Track every piece of feedback

A tracker logged each SME and pilot comment against the exact module or exam item, and whether it was actioned. 25 distinct issues were worked through.

Settle the rules before launch

Eligibility, deadlines, regional exceptions, retests, and escalation paths were written alongside the training, not argued after it.

The solution

Teach only what changed, then test it with situations

Learning path

  • Modules built around the four major changes, plus significant and minor changes
  • Short topic videos and knowledge checks inside each module
  • A quick reference companion for the new framework and terms

Exam

  • 25 scenario-based questions, 90% to pass, open book, resumable
  • Situations, not definitions: “which phase does this activity belong to?”
  • One immediate, non-punitive retest before any class is required

Built to run at global scale

  • Each translation reviewed by a native-speaking consultant before release
  • Exam results flowed automatically through an API into a tracker, with a question-level response log, and digital credentials issued on passing
  • A monthly reminder and marketing campaign running up to the deadline

What the data changed

Assessment data as design feedback

One category scored 70%

A design defect, not a learner defect

Everything else scored 94 to 100%. I rewrote the affected items and revised the module.

The time estimate was wrong

Corrected so people could plan

Pilot data showed the published estimate was unrealistic, especially for non-native English speakers. Communications were updated.

Translation issues showed up in scores

Found by performance, not complaints

A question-level comparison across languages pinpointed items that didn't survive translation, and I reworked them with the regional team.

A platform setting misbehaved

Fixed quickly and in the open

An attempt limit didn't work as labeled. I caught it, corrected affected records, and tightened access, without penalizing learners.

The broader outcome is a repeatable recertification model: a delta-based path the organization can rebuild for the next release, instead of routing every certified professional back through a multi-day class.
